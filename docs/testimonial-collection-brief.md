# Testimonial collection brief

## Metric and boundary

**Done when:** either request can collect a concrete, attributable, permissioned testimonial without inventing a quote or choosing who receives it.

**Budget:** two generic, unsent draft requests and one correction pass. Do not select recipients, send a message, publish a testimonial, or add testimonial UI from this brief.

## Use before any outreach

Choose a recipient only through a separate, approved decision. Before sending either draft, replace bracketed prompts with facts the recipient can verify. Keep the request optional and give the recipient a simple way to decline or correct the context.

For every response, capture these fields before using any text publicly:

| Field | Required record |
| --- | --- |
| Concrete observed outcome | What changed, was learned, delivered, or became possible; include scope and time frame if the person can verify them. |
| Exact quote | The respondent's final words, preserved verbatim. Do not turn a note into a quote without approval. |
| Attribution | Full name, title, organization, and relationship to the work as the respondent wants them shown. |
| Source | Direct reply, approved form response, recorded conversation with confirmation, or another named source. |
| Publication permission | Explicit yes/no for public use, approved quote text, approved attribution, permitted surfaces, and date of approval. |

Store source material and approvals with the appropriate relationship record. Do not put names, email addresses, or unapproved quote text in analytics.

## Draft A — leadership or adoption reference

**Status: UNSENT generic draft**

**Subject:** Request for a short reference about [initiative or period]

Hello [Name],

I’m updating my professional portfolio and would value a short reference if you are comfortable providing one. You saw my work on [initiative] in the context of [your role or working relationship].

Could you describe one concrete outcome you observed? Useful detail could include what the team was able to do differently, an adoption or delivery change you personally saw, or a decision/process that became clearer. Please use only what you can stand behind from your own experience.

If you are willing, please send:

- The exact one-to-three sentence quote you would want published.
- Your preferred attribution: name, title, organization, and relationship to the work.
- The source context you want noted, such as direct collaboration on [initiative].
- Whether you approve public use of that exact quote and attribution, and where: portfolio site, résumé, case study, proposals, or other [specify].

If you would rather not provide a reference, or if I have framed the work incorrectly, a brief no or correction is completely welcome.

Thank you,

Austen

## Draft B — workshop participant reference

**Status: UNSENT generic draft**

**Subject:** Request for a short workshop reflection

Hello [Name],

I’m collecting a small number of optional reflections about [workshop name or topic]. You participated in [date or session], and I would appreciate your perspective only if you are comfortable sharing it.

Could you describe one concrete outcome you experienced or observed after the workshop? For example: something you built or tried, a recurring task you approached differently, a question the session helped you answer, or a result you could verify. Please use your own words and do not feel pressure to make the workshop sound more successful than it was.

If you are willing, please send:

- The exact one-to-three sentence quote you would want published.
- Your preferred attribution: name, title, organization, and workshop-participant context.
- The source context you want noted, such as [workshop name] on [date].
- Whether you approve public use of that exact quote and attribution, and where: portfolio site, workshop page, proposals, or other [specify].

If you would rather decline, anonymize your attribution, or correct the workshop context, please say so plainly.

Thank you,

Austen

## Permission check before publication

Do not publish until all answers are affirmative and saved with the final quote:

- Is this the exact quote you approve for publication?
- Is this the exact attribution you approve?
- Which named public surfaces may use it?
- May the quote be edited for length or grammar? If yes, does the edited version require your final approval?
- Is there an expiry date, organization policy, or other limit on use?
